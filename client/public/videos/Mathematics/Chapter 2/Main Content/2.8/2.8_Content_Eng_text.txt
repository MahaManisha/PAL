2.8.1  •  BACKGROUND
De Moivre's Theorem
Abraham De Moivre (1667–1754)
A mathematician who was among the first to use complex numbers in trigonometry. The formula that 
bears his name helped move trigonometry from pure geometry into algebraic analysis.
De Moivre's Theorem
For any complex number cosθ + i sinθ and any integer n:
(cosθ + i sinθ)ⁿ  =  cos nθ + i sin nθ
This single identity is the engine behind every technique in this section — simplifying powers of complex 
numbers, solving polynomial equations, and finding roots of complex numbers.

--- PAGE 3 ---
2.8.1  •  KEY RESULTS
Corollaries of De Moivre's Theorem
(1)   (cosθ + i sinθ)ⁿ  =  cos nθ + i sin nθ
The theorem itself, for any integer n (positive, negative, or zero).
(2)   (cosθ + i sinθ)⁻ⁿ  =  cos nθ − i sin nθ
Applying the theorem with a negative index.
(3)   (cosθ − i sinθ)ⁿ  =  cos nθ − i sin nθ
The conjugate form raised to the power n.
(4)   sinθ + i cosθ  =  i (cosθ − i sinθ)
A useful rewriting used to bring mixed sin/cos expressions into cis-form before applying the theorem.

2.8.2  •  FINDING nTH ROOTS
Finding the nth Roots of a Complex Number
Definition
For a positive integer n, a complex number ω is called an nth root of z, written ω = z^(1/n), if
ωⁿ = z
Setting up the equation
Let ω = ρ(cosφ + i sinφ)  and  z = r(cosθ + i sinθ) = r[cos(θ+2kπ) + i sin(θ+2kπ)],  k ∈ ℤ
Since ωⁿ = z, De Moivre's theorem gives:
ρⁿ(cos nφ + i sin nφ)  =  r[cos(θ+2kπ) + i sin(θ+2kπ)]
Comparing moduli and arguments:
ρⁿ = r    and    nφ = θ + 2kπ    ⟹ ρ = r^(1/n)  and  φ = (θ+2kπ)/n

--- PAGE 5 ---
2.8.2  •  FORMULA
The nth Roots — Formula and Remarks
For z = r(cosθ + i sinθ), the n distinct nth roots are:
z^(1/n)  =  r^(1/n) [ cos((θ+2kπ)/n) + i sin((θ+2kπ)/n) ],   k = 0, 1, 2, …, n−1
Although k can be any integer, only k = 0,1,…,n−1 give distinct roots — beyond that the roots repeat 
cyclically.
Remark 1 — General form of De Moivre's Theorem
If x is rational, cos(xθ) + i sin(xθ) is one of the values of (cosθ + i sinθ)ˣ.
Remark 2 — Polar form of the unit circle
z = e^(iθ) = cosθ + i sinθ  ⟹ |z|² = cos²θ + sin²θ = 1  ⟹ x²+y² = 1
So z = e^(iθ) traces the unit circle (radius 1, centre at the origin).

2.8.3  •  ROOTS OF UNITY
The nth Roots of Unity
Definition
The n solutions of zⁿ = 1 are called the nth roots of unity.
In polar form:   zⁿ = cos(2kπ) + i sin(2kπ) = e^(i2kπ),   k = 0,1,2,…
Applying the nth-root formula gives:
z  =  cos(2kπ/n) + i sin(2kπ/n)  =  e^(i2kπ/n),    k = 0, 1, 2, …, n−1
Denoting  ω = e^(i2π/n) = cos(2π/n) + i sin(2π/n),  the n roots of unity are exactly
1,  ω,  ω²,  ω³,  …,  ωⁿ⁻¹
(since ωᵏ = e^(i2kπ/n) for k = 0,1,…,n−1).

--- PAGE 7 ---
2.8.3  •  GEOMETRY
Geometric Interpretation of the nth Roots of Unity
Because |ω| = 1, every power ωᵏ also has modulus 1.
•  All n roots  1, ω, ω², …, ωⁿ⁻¹  lie on the unit circle |z| = 1, centred at the origin.
•  Successive roots have arguments differing by exactly 2π/n — so the roots are equally spaced around 
the circle.
•  Plotted in the complex plane, the n roots form the vertices of a regular polygon of n sides inscribed in 
the unit circle.
Example:  the cube roots of unity (n=3) sit at 0°, 120°, 240° on the unit circle, forming an equilateral 
triangle;  the 4th roots (n=4) sit at 0°, 90°, 180°, 270°, forming a square.
This geometric interpretation extends to the general nth roots of any complex number z = r(cosθ+isinθ): 
they lie on a circle of radius r^(1/n), equally spaced by 2π/n.

--- PAGE 8 ---
2.8.3  •  PROPERTIES
Key Properties of the nth Roots of Unity
(1)  Geometric progression
The n roots 1, ω, ω², …, ωⁿ⁻¹ form a G.P . with common ratio ω.
(2)  Sum of the roots is always zero
1 + ω + ω² + ⋯ + ωⁿ⁻¹ = 0     (sum of a G.P . with ratio ω ≠ 1, using ωⁿ=1)
(3)  Product of the roots
1 · ω · ω² ⋯ ωⁿ⁻¹  =  (−1)ⁿ⁻¹
(4)  Regular polygon on the unit circle
All n roots lie on |z| = 1, dividing the circle into n equal arcs and forming a regular n-sided polygon.

2.8.3  •  ROTATION & NOTATION
Rotation Property and the Symbol ω
Rotation by multiplication
For any complex number z, the product  z · e^(iθ)  represents z rotated by θ radians in the counter-
clockwise direction about the origin.
This is the tool used to construct points of a regular polygon (e.g. vertices of an equilateral triangle 
inscribed in a circle) directly from one known vertex.
Notation: the value of ω depends on n
n = 2 :   ω = e^(iπ)             n = 3 :   ω = e^(i2π/3)
n = 4 :   ω = e^(iπ/2)          n = 5 :   ω = e^(i2π/5)
n = k :   ω = e^(i2π/k)   — the general nth root of unity generator

Summary
• De Moivre's Theorem:  (cosθ+isinθ)ⁿ = cos nθ + i sin nθ,  with its 4 corollaries
• nth roots of a complex number:  z^(1/n) = r^(1/n)[cos((θ+2kπ)/n)+isin((θ+2kπ)/n)]
• nth roots of unity:  z = e^(i2kπ/n) = 1, ω, ω², …, ωⁿ⁻¹
• Sum of nth roots of unity = 0;  Product = (−1)ⁿ⁻¹
• Geometric view: roots lie equally spaced on a circle, forming a regular polygon
• Rotation: z·e^(iθ) rotates z by θ radians anticlockwise about the origin
